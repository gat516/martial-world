# Review: the heart-demon trial

**Owner's decision (2026-10-24):** keep the honesty scoring (denials still reduce tempering) and the judging lines. Rec 3 applied: a bigger, visible payoff (50 points a stage, mending to 100%; a calmed trial deepens one stage past it; a milestone card shows each stage before -> after), plus the Clear Heart Pill ([heart-demon](../systems/heart-demon.md#outcome)).

**Verdict: keep with changes.** The writing is the best in the game: short, evocative, built from the player's own record ("312 Spirit Wolves. Do their howls follow you?"). But the "honesty" test is a reading check whose answer is printed in the question. Its reward is about +1.5% power. A few honest answers shame the gardener and the solo kid. Reviewed 2026-10-23 from heart-demon.md and `HeartDemon.luau`; nothing played.

## Why it exists / who it's for
- Arrow: home (seated, at stage 9) into breakthrough. It tempers old foundation grades, so it's the catch-up for an under-explored climb. It feeds Immersion and identity ("story over rules"), and teens like a mirror of how they played.
- It's genuinely good that it's choice-based, has no reflex gate, never blocks, and the 27-character answers fit phone buttons.

## Problems
- **No real decision.** Each question states the record ("You have fallen 3 times..."), and the denial is the answer that contradicts it ("I'm never afraid"). Anyone who reads gets 3 tempers, and anyone who doesn't can retake it ("which is fine", doc). The scoring adds rules but no tension. Worse, the denials are often the heroic, aspirational lines a kid wants to click ("Nothing can touch me.", "I gamble it all."), and those get punished.
- **The payoff is invisible.** 3 stages x 25 points x 0.0002 = +1.5% health and power. The closing line names stages ("Qi Condensation 2 is now Steady") the player doesn't remember. Nothing is felt.
- **It moralises against decided roads.** The owner says both roads are complete, yet the gardener path question is "Were you hiding from the world?" and an honest answer is "Yes. It frightened me." (`HeartDemon.luau:134-136`). Solitude asks "Do you trust no one?" of a kid who may have chat off and no friends online. These are guilt prompts, and they're on the research's avoid list.
- **Few see it.** It's offered at stage 9 only, three times in about 30 h. QC9 comes about 2.4 h of active play in, past most players' D7. That's acceptable for a depth beat, but it shouldn't carry a maintenance rule for every system.
- **A maintenance tax.** "Every new system adds a counter and a question" (doc "Keeping it current") makes every feature cost a writer pass, and the pool already has 13 question types.
- "Gamble" is a word to keep out of a Mild/Select game.

## Metrics
Little effect on D1, a small D7+ immersion beat. It can carry an update (the planned mirror-demon scene is a strong thumbnail). A good-moment like prompt fits right after it.

## Recommendations (ranked)
1. **Score expression, not honesty.** Every finished trial tempers 3. The answers pick a **Dao heart** title (Blade, Garden, Wanderer, Still) shown on the Self card and nameplate: status for teens, nothing to fail. The "denial" replies stay as flavour.
2. **Rewrite the judging lines.** The gardener and solitary questions become curious, not accusing ("Your garden grew wide. What did it teach you?"). Drop "gamble".
3. **Make tempering visible.** On the closing screen, the tempered stages' pips fill with a burst, plus one line "+X% strength". Better: temper by more (50 points), so it's a felt catch-up.
4. Change the AGENTS rule from "must add a question" to "may", and cap the pool at about 8 question types.
5. Build the mirror-copy scene for QR9/FE9 later as the update beat (already planned).
